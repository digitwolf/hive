# Equipment: Flow Hive 2+ (×2)

**Status:** draft — update with actual model/size once ordered.

## What a Flow Hive 2+ is, in Langstroth terms

- A standard Langstroth **brood box** (8-frame "6 Flow frame" or 10-frame
  "7 Flow frame" width) with conventional frames, plus a **Flow super** holding
  the plastic split-cell Flow frames that let you drain honey without opening
  the super.
- Screened bottom board with a slide-out **pest management tray** (doubles as
  a varroa drop board and a place to put the load-cell platform *under*, not in).
- Built-in observation windows on the super end and side, adjustable
  levelling legs, hive-mat / inner cover, gabled roof.
- The Flow super is only useful once the brood box is strong. Year one in
  the PNW: plan on **no Flow harvest**, possibly a small one if the
  blackberry flow is strong.

## Decisions to make before ordering

| Decision | Options | Leaning / rationale |
|----------|---------|---------------------|
| Width | 6-frame (8-fr Langstroth) vs 7-frame (10-fr) | 7-frame: more brood space and standard 10-frame gear (feeders, second brood boxes) is easiest to source locally. |
| Wood | Western red cedar vs araucaria | Cedar weathers better in constant winter damp; costs more. |
| Finish | Tung oil vs paint | Oil on cedar; re-oil annually. Paint if araucaria. |
| Second brood box | Yes / no | **Yes** — PNW colonies typically overwinter better in a double deep or deep + medium. Flow super comes off for winter. |
| Entrance reducer / mouse guard | Included / buy | Buy metal mouse guards; mice move in during October. |
| Queen excluder | Included | Use it; Flow frames with brood in them are a mess. |

## Per-hive kit list (beyond the Flow Hive box)

- Second deep brood box + 8/10 frames with foundation (or foundationless with
  a starter strip; foundation is easier for a first year).
- Hive-top or frame feeder for spring build-up and autumn feeding.
- Insulated top (2" polyiso under the roof) and a moisture quilt or vented
  inner cover for winter — see `../research/hive-heating.md`.
- Ratchet strap over the whole stack (wind).
- Hive stand: cinder blocks + 4×4s, or a metal stand. The Flow legs alone
  are not enough on soft ground; they sink in a Woodinville winter.
- Mite treatment supplies: oxalic acid (dribble or vapor — vaporizer if
  going that route), Formic Pro or Apiguard for summer. Mite wash cup
  (alcohol wash) for counts.

Shared: smoker, hive tool (J-hook), bee suit/jacket, bee brush, frame grip,
uncapping tools not needed (Flow), a 3–4 L food-grade bucket + Flow tube
for harvest, sugar (lots), spare queen cage.

## Instrumentation impact

- Load cells go **under the hive**, between the stand and the bottom board.
  The Flow Hive's leveling legs complicate this; the plan in
  `../../hardware/README.md` is a separate scale platform the whole hive sits
  on, legs removed or retracted.
- Temperature probes go through the inner cover into the top of the brood
  nest; the Flow inner cover has a feed hole that can be re-used for the
  cable gland.
- The observation windows are handy for verifying sensor readings without
  opening the hive.
